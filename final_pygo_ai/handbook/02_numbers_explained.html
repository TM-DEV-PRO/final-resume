Chapter 2 — Every resume number, explained

How to use this chapter
• Each number has five parts: what it measures, where it comes from (arithmetic or source), why it mattered, how to say it, and what not to say.
• Interviewers love to ask "how did you measure that?". If you can explain the source in one sentence, the number becomes believable.
• Read the "trap pairs" in section 7 last. Those are the numbers people mix up.

Tags used below. - MEASURED: there is a log, benchmark, document or code that shows it. - ESTIMATED: arithmetic you can show on a whiteboard. Say "roughly" and show the math. - HISTORICAL: from your past work records at an earlier company. Say it plainly, and attribute business outcomes to the team that measured them. - OFF-PDF: true, but not printed on the resume. Use it only when it helps an answer.

2. Impact Analytics — AssortSmart (May 2026 to Present)

Agentic

348k article-seasons · MEASURED - What: How many product-in-a-season items the Keep/Drop engine scores. - Source: The scored volume from the Keep/Drop runs. - Why it mattered: It's far more than planners can review by hand, which is why automation plus explanations is valuable. - Say: "It evaluates about 348 thousand article-seasons." - Don't: Confuse it with the 1.6M catalog (all product × season combinations, OFF-PDF).

88k items per pass · MEASURED - What: The batch size of one scoring run. - Why: Sized to fit memory and LLM provider rate limits. Also the unit that checkpoints and circuit breakers protect. - Say: "About 88 thousand items per pass." - OFF-PDF: about $100 per pass in token spend.

7 AI lenses · MEASURED - What: Seven active LLM "questions" (colour cannibalisation, size curve, channel divergence, multi-season consistency, trend velocity, discount dependency, margin resilience). - Say: "Seven structured lenses, each only run when the article has data for it."

2.11B-row ClickHouse master · MEASURED - What: The size of the main fact table the agents' context comes from. - Why: At that size, letting an LLM query it is dangerous and expensive. That's why it gets JSON. - Don't: Mix it with 4.09B (a rollup table in the copier story).

3 attempts (Ask Iris evaluator) · MEASURED (code constant MAX_ATTEMPTS = 3) - Why: Enough to recover from a bad first answer, low enough to cap cost and stop loops.

300 cases / 80% gate · MEASURED (config gold_min_accuracy: 0.8, cases file) - What: A 300-case proxy evaluation set. CI blocks promotion of a config that scores below 80%. - Say: "Promotion is gated at 80% on a 300-case proxy set in CI." - Don't: "80% accuracy in production for all tenants."

73% lower cost at 100% coverage · MEASURED (gold-200 benchmark) - What: On a separate 200-case benchmark, the chosen model cost 73% less than the previous mini model and completed all 200 cases (100% coverage = no pending rows). - Why: Running across all clients every season, cost is what decides whether it's viable. - Don't: Say the 300-case set measured 73%.

74% deterministic baseline · MEASURED (same 200-case benchmark) - What: Accuracy of the free rule-based score (threshold 0.65) on that 200-case set. - Why: It's the bar the AI must beat. The blended LLM scored about 63%, below it, so blend weights were frozen. - Say: "When the blend lost to the 74% rule, we froze the weights instead of tuning to the test."

Core

10k peak RPS · on the PDF - What: Peak requests per second the Go/Gin platform serves. - How to explain the source: name the actual source you saw (for example a Datadog request-rate graph or a load test). Only say the one that's true for you. - Don't: Turn it into an average or an uptime SLA.

15.5× (189 s to 12 s on 250M rows) · MEASURED (benchmark harness) - What: The same planner pivot on a 250-million-row test: 189 seconds on the old PostgreSQL path, 12.3 seconds on ClickHouse rollups (rounded to 12 on the PDF). - Arithmetic: 189 ÷ 12.3 ≈ 15.4. The harness reported about 15.5×. If someone checks, "about 15 times faster" is safe. - Why: A 3-minute screen is unusable. 12 seconds is workable for a heavy grid. - Say: "From 189 seconds to 12 seconds on a 250-million-row test." - Don't: "Sub-second", or "because we fixed the OOM".

170 GB OOM · MEASURED - What: Estimated memory a whole-season weekly GROUP BY needed. It failed even with 80 GB and 200 GB caps. - Fix knobs (OFF-PDF, true): one fiscal week per insert, 55 GB memory cap, 16 GB spill, 8 threads, 4 weeks in flight. Product week ≈ 11 GB, attribute week > 40 GB. - Don't: Link it to the 15.5×. OOM = building tables. 15.5× = reading them.

~344k rows/s · MEASURED (live snapshot) - What: Combined copy speed of the Go pump, two season streams of about 150k–190k rows/s each. - Don't: Call it TPS or RPS. It's rows per second of bulk data.

4.09B rows · MEASURED - What: Largest table copied: attribute weekly, two seasons (2,779,317,472 + 1,311,108,195). - Arithmetic (ESTIMATED): 4.09B ÷ 344k ≈ 11,900 s ≈ 3.3 hours at that rate. - Don't: Say the cutover finished. It was measured in flight.

500k-row batches · MEASURED - What: Rows per insert batch in the pump, with double buffering (read the next 500k while sending the current one).

OFF-PDF but true: 100% Go statement coverage with race detection across 1,200+ tests (on LinkedIn). 839M rows in product weekly for the same two seasons. Six rollup tables.

3. Uber via EPAM (Jul 2024 to May 2026)

FRM Scoping Platform

70% (from 14 days to 3 days) - What: Quarterly reconciliation time. The calendar went from about 14 days to about 3. - Source: The technical design doc set a 70% reduction in manual ingestion and reconciliation time as the goal. The PDF states it as the outcome. Defend the PDF. If pressed: "the design doc framed that cut and that's what the platform delivered for the close." - If someone does the math: 14 → 3 is about a 79% calendar cut. Say "I quote the conservative 70% figure, which is the reduction in manual reconciliation effort that the project measured itself against." - Why: The close is time-boxed and audit-facing. Days saved is analyst time and risk saved.

$340M materiality threshold - What: The dollar amount above which a misstatement would matter to someone reading Uber's financials. On the Q4 2025 close, group materiality was $340M (residual threshold $170M, verbal). - Why: Every scoping decision ("is this line item in scope for audit?") compares against it. - Say: "Against a $340 million materiality threshold." Present it as that close's value, not a fixed constant.

19M raw GL rows - What: General-ledger rows covered by the Sheets-to-MySQL migration. - If asked about volume per quarter: individual quarterly GL extracts were much smaller (tens of thousands of rows). The value of the system is correctness, hierarchy and auditability, not big-data throughput. Don't invent a breakdown of the 19M beyond "raw GL rows migrated off Sheets".

36 endpoints (FastAPI) - What: API endpoints shipped for the scoping workflow. - If they drill in: about 31 functional routes in the scoping service handler, plus the collaboration-service APIs (comments, notifications) from the design doc.

L1–L4 FSLI hierarchy - What: Four levels of Financial Statement Line Items, for example Assets → Current assets → Receivables → Trade receivables.

8-table normalized schema - What: The core scoping schema on Uber's SOADB (MySQL). If they've seen code-level detail: the scoping service has 11 ORM models including recon and helper tables.

Led 3 engineers - What: Tech lead of a 3-person EPAM pod on the ORM migration. Not a people manager. - Don't: Merge with "mentored 2" (that's Masters India).

100% statement coverage - What: Every line in the migrated module executed by tests. Statement coverage doesn't prove every behaviour is correct, so pair it with "plus tests for the aliasing bug class".

50% delta-variance check - What: A state-machine gate. A line item can't move forward (for example to Closed) if its period-over-period change exceeds 50% without review.

Menu Ingestion (Uber Eats)

From 24 hours to 2 hours · HISTORICAL - What: Restaurant onboarding time for the menu. (24 − 2) ÷ 24 ≈ 92% faster.

$600K per year · HISTORICAL, arithmetic - Derivation: about $2 per menu in manual or third-party cost × 30,000 menus × 12 months = $720K. Floored to $600K to be conservative. - Say: "Roughly $2 a menu, 30 thousand a month, that's about $720K a year. I quote $600K to be conservative."

30K+ menus per month · HISTORICAL - Arithmetic: about 1,000 a day.

98% field fidelity · HISTORICAL, offline evaluation - What: Share of extracted fields (name, price, description, category...) that matched the labelled answer in an offline evaluation set. - Always say: "offline", or "on our evaluation set".

95%+ extraction success · HISTORICAL - What: Share of scrape-and-extract jobs that completed. The baseline before hardening was roughly 60–65% (ESTIMATED).

OFF-PDF, prep only: Kafka peak of about 200–500 events per second (ESTIMATED from fleet-run bursts). Don't put it on the resume.

Document Compliance (Uber Mobility ANZ)

99.9% compliance, 20 hours per week saved · HISTORICAL - What: Share of driver and vehicle documents meeting local authority rules after automation, and manual review time removed each week. - Don't: Call it Uber Eats.

1M+ daily government submissions · HISTORICAL - Arithmetic (ESTIMATED): 1,000,000 ÷ 86,400 ≈ 11.6, so about 12 per second on average. Filing-deadline peaks roughly 8–10× that.

100K+ concurrent imports · HISTORICAL - What: Rows in bulk imports processed concurrently with idempotency keys.

Mentored 2 engineers · HISTORICAL
